## 1. Mathematical Foundations of Stiefel Manifold Optimization

Let the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal $K$-frames in $\mathbb{R}^D$ be defined by:
$$\mathcal{M} = St(D, K) = \{ X \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K} : X^T X = I_K \}$$
where $D \gg K \ge 1$. The tangent space at $X \in St(D, K)$ is given by:
$$T_X St(D, K) = \{ \Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K} : X^T \Delta + \Delta^T X = 0 \}$$

Under the canonical Euclidean metric $\langle \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \rangle_e = \operatorname{tr}(\Delta_1^T \Delta_2)$ or the canonical Riemannian metric $\langle \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \rangle_c = \operatorname{tr}\left(\Delta_1^T (I_D - \frac{1}{2} X X^T) \Delta_2\right)$, the Riemannian gradient $\operatorname{grad} f(X)$ of a smooth objective $f: St(D, K) \to \mathbb{R}$ with Euclidean gradient $G = \nabla f(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}$ is:
$$\operatorname{grad} f(X) = G - X G^T X = (I_D - X X^T) G + X \operatorname{skew}(X^T G)$$
where $\operatorname{skew}(A) = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^T)$.

### 1.1 The Low-Rank Cayley Transform and Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury Form
The continuous geodesic flow or quasi-geodesic curve is generated by the skew-symmetric velocity operator:
$$W = G X^T - X G^T \in \mathfrak{so}(D)$$
Because $W$ has rank at most $2K$, we factor $W = U V^T$ where:
$$U = \begin{bmatrix} G & X \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times 2K}, \quad V = \begin{bmatrix} X & -G \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times 2K}$$
The Cayley transform retracts along the manifold via:
$$Y(\tau) = \operatorname{cay}(\tau W) X = \left( I_D - \frac{\tau}{2} W \right)^{-1} \left( I_D + \frac{\tau}{2} W \right) X$$
Applying the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury (SMW) theorem transforms the $D \times D$ dense inversion into a $2K \times 2K$ core solve:
$$Y(\tau) = X - \tau U M^{-1} V^T X$$
where the core interaction matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{2K \times 2K}$ is:
$$M = I_{2K} + \frac{\tau}{2} V^T U = I_{2K} + \frac{\tau}{2} \begin{bmatrix} X^T G & X^T X \\ -G^T G & -G^T X \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} I_K + \frac{\tau}{2} X^T G & \frac{\tau}{2} I_K \\ -\frac{\tau}{2} G^T G & I_K - \frac{\tau}{2} G^T X \end{bmatrix}$$

## 2. Block $LDL^T$ Factorization with Rook / Bunch-Kaufman Pivoting for Perturbed Skew-Symmetric Systems

### 2.1 The Generalized Perturbed System
In second-order Riemannian optimization (Riemannian Newton-CG, Riemannian Trust-Region, or natural metric deformations), higher-order metric curvature and regularization introduce symmetric perturbations:
$$M = I_{2K} + \alpha \Omega + \alpha^2 Q$$
where:
- $\Omega = S - S^T \in \mathfrak{so}(2K)$ is skew-symmetric ($\Omega^T = -\Omega$).
- $Q = Q^T \succeq 0 \in \mathbb{R}^{2K \times 2K}$ is symmetric positive semi-definite.
- $\alpha = \frac{\tau}{2} > 0$ is the step parameter.

### 2.2 Numerical Instability of Partial LU Pivoting
Standard Gaussian Elimination with partial pivoting ($P M = L U$) ignores the Hamiltonian / skew-symmetric spectral symmetry. As $\kappa(M) \gg 1$ (which occurs when $\tau \|G\|_F \gg 1$ or when $G$ has ill-conditioned singular spectrum), partial pivoting encounters:
1. **Element Growth:** Growth factor $\rho_n = \frac{\max_{i,j,k} |a_{ij}^{(k)}|}{\max_{i,j} |a_{ij}|} \le 2^{2K-1}$, which degrades to catastrophic loss of precision for $2K \ge 64$.
2. **Schur Complement Cancellation:** In computing $S = M_{22} - M_{21} M_{11}^{-1} M_{12}$, if $M_{11}$ is near-singular (e.g. purely imaginary eigenvalues $\pm i \omega$ close to 0 with small real parts), scalar division produces floating-point overflow and catastrophic loss of orthogonality:
$$\|Y(\tau)^T Y(\tau) - I_K\|_2 = \mathcal{O}\left( \mathbf{u} \cdot \kappa(M)^2 \right)$$

### 2.3 Indefinite Block Factorization $P M P^T = L D L^T$
To guarantee backward stability, $M$ is symmetrized/anti-symmetrized or solved via an augmented symmetric indefinite formulation. For a symmetric indefinite or structured matrix, the block $LDL^T$ decomposition computes:
$$P M P^T = L D L^T$$
where:
- $P$ is a permutation matrix.
- $L$ is unit lower triangular with $|L_{ij}| \le \mu^{-1}$.
- $D = \operatorname{diag}(D_1, D_2, \dots, D_m)$ is block diagonal with blocks $D_i$ of size $1 \times 1$ or $2 \times 2$.

#### SOTA Pivot Selection: Bunch-Kaufman vs Rook Pivoting
1. **Bunch-Kaufman (BK):**
   Let $\nu_0 = \max_{i \neq 1} |M_{i1}| = |M_{r1}|$. If $|M_{11}| \ge \mu \nu_0$ (where $\mu = \frac{1+\sqrt{17}}{8} \approx 0.6404$), use a $1 \times 1$ pivot at $(1,1)$.
   Otherwise, find $\nu_r = \max_{i \neq r} |M_{ir}|$. If $|M_{11}| \nu_r \ge \mu \nu_0^2$, use a $1 \times 1$ pivot at $(1,1)$.
   If $|M_{rr}| \ge \mu \nu_r$, use a $1 \times 1$ pivot at $(r,r)$.
   Otherwise, perform a 2-row/column swap and use the $2 \times 2$ pivot:
   $$D_1 = \begin{bmatrix} M_{11} & M_{1r} \\ M_{r1} & M_{rr} \end{bmatrix}$$
2. **Rook Pivoting (Ashcraft-Grimes-Lewis / Foster):**
   Rook pivoting alternates row and column searches until finding an element $(r, c)$ such that:
   $$|M_{rc}| = \max_i |M_{ic}| = \max_j |M_{rj}|$$
   - **Advantage:** Unlike Bunch-Kaufman, where multiplier bounds can degrade in off-diagonal solves, Rook pivoting strictly guarantees:
   $$\max_{i,j} |L_{ij}| \le \mu^{-1} \approx 1.5616, \quad \rho_{2K} \le (1 + \mu^{-1})^{2K-1} \approx 2.57^{2K-1}$$

### 2.4 Preservation of Skew-Conjugate Eigenvalues in $2 \times 2$ Blocks
The eigenvalues of $\alpha \Omega$ are purely imaginary conjugate pairs $\pm i \alpha \omega_k$. Under perturbation $\alpha^2 Q$, the eigenvalues become $\delta_k \pm i \alpha \omega_k$ ($\delta_k > 0$).
By selecting $2 \times 2$ pivot blocks:
$$D_k = \begin{bmatrix} 1 + \alpha^2 Q_{11} & \alpha \omega_k + \alpha^2 Q_{12} \\ -\alpha \omega_k + \alpha^2 Q_{21} & 1 + \alpha^2 Q_{22} \end{bmatrix}$$
The determinant is:
$$\det(D_k) = (1 + \alpha^2 Q_{11})(1 + \alpha^2 Q_{22}) + \alpha^2 \omega_k^2 - \alpha^4 Q_{12} Q_{21} \ge 1 + \alpha^2 \omega_k^2 > 0$$
**Theorem (Schur Stability):** Because $\det(D_k) \ge 1$, $D_k^{-1}$ is uniformly bounded:
$$\|D_k^{-1}\|_2 \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(D_k)}} \le 1$$
The updated Schur complement $S_k = M_{22} - M_{21} D_k^{-1} M_{12}$ satisfies:
$$\kappa(S_k) \le \kappa(M) \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\alpha^2 \|Q\|_2}{1 + \alpha^2 \omega_{min}^2}\right)$$
This completely eliminates the catastrophic singularity found in standard scalar LU factorizations.

## 3. Shifted-Inverse and Matrix-Free Krylov Solvers on $St(D, K)$ for $K > 32$

### 3.1 The $O(K^3)$ Scalability Barrier
When $K$ grows to $64, 128, 256, 1024$ in massive-channel latent manifolds:
- Dense formation of $V^T U$ requires $4 D K^2$ FLOPs.
- Direct factorization of $M \in \mathbb{R}^{2K \times 2K}$ requires $\frac{2}{3}(2K)^3 = \frac{16}{3} K^3$ FLOPs.
- Total memory bandwidth for storing $U, V$ is $4 D K \times \text{sizeof(float64)}$.

For $D = 10^6, K = 256$, direct dense solve requires $\approx 2.6 \times 10^{11}$ FLOPs per step and 4 GB RAM transfers per iteration.

### 3.2 Matrix-Free Operator Formulation
The linear system to solve during retraction is:
$$\left( I_D - \frac{\tau}{2} W \right) Y = \left( I_D + \frac{\tau}{2} W \right) X$$
Letting $Z = Y - X \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}$, the system simplifies to:
$$\mathcal{A}(Z) \equiv Z - \frac{\tau}{2} W(Z) = \tau W(X)$$
where the operator action $\mathcal{A}: \mathbb{R}^{D \times K} \to \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}$ is computed matrix-free in $O(D K)$ FLOPs without forming $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
$$W(Z) = G (X^T Z) - X (G^T Z)$$

### 3.3 Shifted Spectrum and Krylov Acceleration
The eigenvalues of the linear operator $\mathcal{W}(Z) = G(X^T Z) - X(G^T Z)$ satisfy:
$$\sigma(\mathcal{W}) \subset i [-\omega_{max}, \omega_{max}] \cup \{0\}, \quad \omega_{max} \le 2 \|G\|_2$$
Thus, the operator $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{I} - \frac{\tau}{2} \mathcal{W}$ has spectrum confined strictly to the shifted vertical line:
$$\sigma(\mathcal{A}) \subset \left\{ 1 + i \frac{\tau}{2} \lambda_j : \lambda_j \in [-\omega_{max}, \omega_{max}] \right\}$$

#### Shifted Skew-Lanczos Bidiagonalization
Because $\mathcal{W} = -\mathcal{W}^*$, the Krylov subspace $\mathcal{K}_m(\mathcal{A}, R_0) = \operatorname{span}\{R_0, \mathcal{A} R_0, \dots, \mathcal{A}^{m-1} R_0\}$ reduces from a full Hessenberg reduction to a short-recurrence **Skew-Hermitian Lanczos** process:
$$\beta_{j+1} v_{j+1} = \mathcal{W}(v_j) + \beta_j v_{j-1}$$
Requiring only 3 vector frames in memory ($3 \times D \times K$ floats) rather than storing all $m$ Arnoldi basis vectors.

#### Deflated GMRES-DR (Recycled Krylov Subspace)
For ill-conditioned gradients ($\kappa(G) \gg 1$), small singular modes produce clustered eigenvalues near $1 \pm i \epsilon$. Recycled GMRES (GMRES-DR) retains $k$ harmonic Ritz vectors $\tilde{Y}_k = [y_1, \dots, y_k]$ across consecutive optimization steps:
$$\mathcal{P}_{deflate} = I - \mathcal{A} \tilde{Y}_k (\tilde{Y}_k^T \mathcal{A}^T \mathcal{A} \tilde{Y}_k)^{-1} \tilde{Y}_k^T \mathcal{A}^T$$
**Theorem (Condition Bounding):** The deflated operator condition number satisfies:
$$\kappa_2(\mathcal{A}_{\text{deflated}}) \le \frac{\sqrt{1 + \frac{\tau^2}{4} \omega_{k+1}^2}}{\sqrt{1 + \frac{\tau^2}{4} \omega_{2K}^2}} \le 1 + \frac{\tau}{2} (\omega_{k+1} - \omega_{2K})$$
independent of the ambient dimension $D$.

## 4. Nguyen-Sommer (SIAM 2025) Parallel Transport vs Higham-Al-Mohy Exponential Actions

### 4.1 Differential Geometry of Parallel Transport on $St(D, K)$
Let $X(t)$ be a geodesic on $St(D, K)$ originating at $X_0 \in St(D, K)$ with initial velocity $\Delta \in T_{X_0} St(D, K)$. Let $\Delta = X_0 A + Q R$ be the thin QR-decomposition of the normal component, where $A = X_0^T \Delta \in \mathfrak{so}(K)$ and $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}$ with $Q^T Q = I_K, X_0^T Q = 0$.

The geodesic equation on $St(D, K)$ under the canonical metric ($\alpha = 1/2$) has the exact closed form:
$$X(t) = \begin{bmatrix} X_0 & Q \end{bmatrix} \exp\left( t \begin{bmatrix} A & -R^T \\ R & 0 \end{bmatrix} \right) \begin{bmatrix} I_K \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Let $V_0 \in T_{X_0} St(D, K)$ be a tangent vector to be parallel-transported along $X(t)$ ($\nabla_{\dot{X}(t)} V(t) = 0$).

### 4.2 The Nguyen-Sommer (SIMAX 2025) Theorem
Du Nguyen and Stefan Sommer (*Parallel Transport on Matrix Manifolds and Exponential Action*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl., 46(4), 2025, DOI: 10.1137/24M1684189) established that for naturally reductive metrics and their $\alpha$-deformations on Stiefel manifolds, the parallel transport $V(t) = \mathcal{P}_{0 \to t}(V_0)$ can be computed **without solving differential equations**, using the action of a $(2K \times 2K)$ matrix exponential:

$$V(t) = \begin{bmatrix} X(t) & Q(t) \end{bmatrix} \exp\left( t \mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(A, R) \right) \begin{bmatrix} X_0^T V_0 \\ Q^T V_0 \end{bmatrix} + (I - X_0 X_0^T - Q Q^T) V_0$$
where $\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(A, R) \in \mathfrak{so}(2K)$ is the skew-symmetric generator matrix:
$$\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(A, R) = \begin{bmatrix} (1 - \alpha) A & -R^T \\ R & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

This breakthrough reduces the complexity of exact parallel transport from $\mathcal{O}(D^3)$ or stiff ODE integrations down to evaluating the matrix exponential action $\exp(t \mathcal{M}) v$ on a $2K$-dimensional vector!

#### Exact Error Bounds Comparison

1. **Padé $[13/13]$ Approximation with Scaling $s$:**
   $$\mathcal{R}_{13,13}(Z) = [P_{13}(-Z)]^{-1} P_{13}(Z), \quad E = \left( \mathcal{R}_{13,13}(2^{-s} t \mathcal{M}) \right)^{2^s}$$
   The truncation error is bounded by:
   $$\| e^{t \mathcal{M}} - E \|_2 \le \Delta_{13} \frac{\|2^{-s} t \mathcal{M}\|^{27}}{(27)!} 2^s \le \mathbf{u} = 2^{-53} \approx 1.11 \times 10^{-16}$$
   Because $\mathcal{M} \in \mathfrak{so}(2K)$, $\mathcal{R}_{13,13}(Z)$ is a Cayley-type rational map satisfying $\mathcal{R}_{13,13}(Z)^{-1} = \mathcal{R}_{13,13}(-Z) = \mathcal{R}_{13,13}(Z)^T$.
   **Crucial Stability Result:** The Padé approximant lies *algebraically on $SO(2K)$*. Its isometric drift is strictly bounded by roundoff:
   $$\| E^T E - I_{2K} \|_2 \le c(13) \cdot s \cdot \mathbf{u} \approx 1.4 \times 10^{-15}$$

2. **Higham-Al-Mohy (2011/2025) Taylor Action:**
   $$y = \left( \sum_{j=0}^m \frac{(s^{-1} t \mathcal{M})^j}{j!} \right)^s v$$
   Where $m \in [1, 55]$ and $s \ge 1$ are selected dynamically via:
   $$\theta_m = \max \left\{ \theta : \sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty} \frac{\theta^{k-m}}{k!} \le \mathbf{u} \right\}, \quad s = \left\lceil \frac{\alpha_{p}(\mathcal{M})}{\theta_m} \right\rceil$$
   While the forward error $\|y - e^{t\mathcal{M}} v\|_2 \le \mathbf{u} \|v\|_2$ is backward stable, **truncated Taylor polynomials are not orthogonal matrices**. Thus:
   $$\|y\|_2^2 - \|v\|_2^2 = v^T \left( T_m(t\mathcal{M})^T T_m(t\mathcal{M}) - I \right) v \neq 0$$
   Norm drift is $\delta = \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{\|t\mathcal{M}\|^{m+1}}{(m+1)!} \right)$. When parallel-transporting tensorial covariance frames in multi-agent LatentMAS over 1,000 hops, this drift accumulates as $\sqrt{H} \delta$.
   **Remedy:** SOTA 2026 mandates a single Givens / Householder normalizer step after Higham-Al-Mohy action evaluation:
   $$y_{\text{exact}} = y \cdot \frac{\|v\|_2}{\|y\|_2}$$
